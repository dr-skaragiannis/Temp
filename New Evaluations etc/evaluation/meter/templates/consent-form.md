## 1. Information sheet

**Study.** MeshCrowd: measuring how people use a passive RF crowd- and vehicle-sensing app on
their own phone (usability and permission flow).

**Who runs it.** [Name(s) of the experimenter], supervised by [Supervisor], Department of
[Department], Ionian University, Plateia Tsirigoti 7, Corfu, Greece. Contact: [email] / [phone].

**What you will be asked to do.** One session of about [30–45] minutes on a [handset model]
running Android [version], with the MeshCrowd [campaign build / measurement build] installed:
1. eight short tasks on the phone itself (install/launch, grant the permissions the app asks for,
   start a sensing session, read the crowd estimate, hold a session for a walk, read the score and
   badges, export the session log, stop the app);
2. three short questionnaires afterwards (10 + 2 + 6 items each, all on a 1–5 scale, plus a
   workload rating);
3. permission to keep the app's own session log for that run.

**What is recorded.**
- For each task: whether it was completed, how long it took, how many tries, whether you asked for
  help, and whether you ended up on the screen the task was about.
- The questionnaire answers, tied to a **participant code you choose** (e.g. `P07`), not to your name.
- Handset model, Android version, and app version.
- The app's session log: radio signal strength readings and scan timings from the phone's own
  Wi-Fi/Bluetooth interfaces, with timestamps.

**What is not recorded.** No audio, no photographs, no camera or microphone use — the app does not
use them at any point. No contact details, no names, no account identifiers, no location history, no
addresses of nearby devices: the app keeps no persistent beacon identifiers, and the log we retain is
checked for that before it is stored. Your personal phone number, accounts, and installed apps are
not inspected or copied.

**Risks and discomfort.** Minimal. The sensing run keeps the radio active, so the phone may become
warm and the battery may drain faster than usual; we keep a charger available and stop the run on
request. Granting the location/bluetooth permissions may feel intrusive; you may decline any
permission, in which case we record that you declined and what the app then did — that is itself a
result.

**Benefits.** None direct. Your answers improve the interface and let us report honestly what a
phone can and cannot do in this role.

**Afterwards.** You get a copy of this form. The data are stored on a [full-disk-encrypted] machine,
kept for [X] years after publication, then deleted. An anonymised version of the task table may be
published with the paper; it contains no names, faces, voices, or device identifiers.

**Withdrawal.** You may stop at any point without giving a reason, and you may ask us to delete
everything recorded from your session up to [date]; the study contact above is the way to do it.

**Questions.** [Name], [email]. [Optional: institutional contact for research-ethics concerns.]
